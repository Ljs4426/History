Lesson 55 Unit 8 · Pursuing American Ideals

A Shift to the Right Under Reagan

How did the Reagan Revolution impact the nation?

Three Examples
01 · 1981

Economic Recovery Tax Act (ERTA)

Reagan's 1981 tax cuts dropped the top rate from 70% all the way to 28% by 1986 (TCI). Supply-side economics: cut taxes on rich people, they invest more, benefits trickle down to everyone. GDP grew and the stock market roughly doubled over his presidency (Miller Center, 2024).

Other Perspective Supporters say the growth proves it worked. Critics point out the top 1%'s income share started climbing sharply in the early 80s and never stopped, so most of the gains went to people who were already rich (Tax Policy Center, 2023).
02 · 1981–1989

Deregulation: Banking, Airlines, and Broadcasting

Reagan loosened rules on banks, airlines, and broadcasting. Short-term: cheaper flights, more TV channels. Long-term: savings and loan banks started gambling deposits on risky investments. The Garn-St. Germain Act (1982) was the key move — it let S&L banks invest in commercial real estate and junk bonds instead of just home mortgages, which is what they were originally chartered to do. When those bets went bad in the late 1980s the FDIC didn't have the reserves to cover it and Congress had to create the Resolution Trust Corporation to liquidate the failed banks.

Other Perspective Over 1,000 savings and loans collapsed. Taxpayer bailout: $124 billion. Supporters say deregulation made the economy more competitive. Critics say it caused exactly the crisis it was supposed to prevent, and regular people paid for it.
03 · 1981–1989

Military Buildup and a Tripling National Debt

Defense spending nearly doubled: $158B in 1981 to $300B by 1989. Cut taxes + spend more on the military + don't cut anything else = the national debt tripled: $900 billion to $2.8 trillion (Miller Center, 2024).

Other Perspective Conservatives say the buildup pressured the Soviets into collapse and it was worth it. Critics note that running massive peacetime deficits became normal after Reagan, and the U.S. hasn't run a sustained surplus since.
Modern Connection: ICED Method

The Tax Policy Center (Urban Institute + Brookings) tracks how Reagan's tax changes affected income distribution. Their data shows the top 1%'s share of national income roughly doubled between 1980 and 2020 ("Historical Highest Marginal Income Tax Rates," Tax Policy Center, 2023). Cutting the top rate from 70% to 28% meant people with the most capital kept the most gains. Deregulation made that worse for anyone outside the financial sector. Every argument you hear now about taxing the wealthy started in those years. What gets called a "normal" tax rate was decided in 1981 and it's barely moved since.

Importance & Impact

Reagan didn't just change policy, he changed what counted as normal. Tax cuts, deregulation, and big deficits all became politically acceptable after him, for both parties. Both parties spent the next 40 years treating those as the floor. You can't make sense of modern arguments about taxes or the national debt without knowing what happened in 1981.
