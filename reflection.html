Reflection Unit 8 · Pursuing American Ideals

Personal Reflection

What did I learn, what surprised me, and how does this connect to my life today?

My Reflection

What Went Well

Reagan was the easiest lesson to start with because his decisions show up everywhere. You can't have a conversation about taxes or the national debt without ending up back in 1981. Once I understood supply-side economics the other examples clicked. The Cold War lesson was harder since so much happened between 1981 and 1991, and narrowing it to three examples without losing the point was frustrating.

The Supreme Court cases were my favorite part because they're actually relevant to my life. The Mahanoy case is literally about what you can post on Snapchat without your school punishing you. That didn't feel like homework.

What I'd Change

I'd spend less time on the design and more time on the analysis. I got into building the site and had to rush through the modern connections more than I wanted to. The Mearsheimer citation for the Cold War lesson specifically, I think I could have explained the Ukraine connection better if I'd given it more time.

I also would have started the research earlier. I kept thinking I knew the Reagan stuff already from class notes, but when I actually went to write the ICED section I realized I needed real numbers and specific citations, and finding those took longer than expected.

What I Learned from Other Presentations

Looking at other presentations I realized I didn't connect my three lessons to each other much. L55, L56, and L59 are all part of the same bigger question about what the government is supposed to do and who it protects. I treated them as separate things and they're not really. I also saw presentations using timelines that made the Cold War sequence much easier to follow, which I wish I had done.

How I See Myself in This

What caught me off guard was how much of this stuff is still running. The tax and deficit arguments from 1981 are the same ones happening right now. Russia is pushing back against NATO basically the same way it was in the 90s, except people are dying because of it now. When Supreme Court cases come up about speech or voting, they all connect back to things decided before I was born.

I thought history was about finished events. A lot of it is still going, just wearing different clothes. The wealth gap that started widening in the 80s shows up in every conversation about student loans and housing today. The arms treaties that collapsed are part of why nuclear risk is back in the news. The redistricting cases from 2019 are going to affect how much my vote matters when I'm old enough to cast one.

So how do I see myself as someone engaged in the economy and society? More engaged than I expected, honestly. You can't opt out of a tax system or a foreign policy. You just have to understand what's happening well enough to know what you think about it.
