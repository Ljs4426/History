Unit 8 Pursuing American Ideals

Conclusion

What do these three lessons actually add up to?

Lesson 55

Reagan Reset the Baseline

The tax cuts, deregulation, and deficits from 1981 didn't just change policy for eight years — they changed what both parties treat as normal. The top income tax rate has never gone back above 40%. The national debt has never come back down. Every argument about taxes or the size of government since then has started from assumptions Reagan put in place (TCI; Miller Center, 2024).

Lesson 56

The Cold War Didn't Really End

The Berlin Wall fell and the USSR dissolved, but the tensions didn't disappear — they just paused. NATO expanded eastward, the INF Treaty collapsed in 2019, and Russia invaded Ukraine in 2022. The decisions made between 1989 and 1999 about what the post-Cold War world would look like are still playing out right now (Mearsheimer, Foreign Affairs, 2022).

Lesson 59

Rights Get Re-Litigated Every Generation

Mahanoy, Bostock, and Rucho all came from the same Constitution that's been in place since 1789. The words don't change — what changes is who's asking the question and what technology or social context they're asking it in. A cheerleader's Snapchat, a gay worker's employment status, a gerrymandered district map. The rights were always there. Someone had to fight to apply them (Oyez, 2021; TCI).

The Through-Line

All three lessons point at the same thing: decisions made decades ago don't stay in the past. Reagan's tax policy shows up in every debate about the wealth gap today. The Cold War's messy ending shows up in the news every time Russia moves troops. The Supreme Court cases from 2019 to 2021 will shape what the next generation can say online, who gets workplace protection, and how much their vote actually counts. History isn't a list of finished events — it's the explanation for why things are the way they are right now.
