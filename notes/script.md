## LESSON 55 — REAGAN
*(navigate to lesson-55.html)*

"Lesson 55 is about the Reagan Revolution and how it shifted the country to the right. I picked three examples.

First is the 1981 tax cuts — Reagan dropped the top income tax rate from 70% all the way down to 28%. The idea was supply-side economics: cut taxes on the wealthy, they invest more, and that money trickles down. GDP did grow, but the Tax Policy Center's data shows the top 1% basically doubled their share of national income by 2020, so the trickle-down part is debatable.

Second is deregulation. Reagan loosened rules on banks, airlines, and broadcasting. Cheaper flights, more channels — but the savings and loan industry collapsed and taxpayers paid about 124 billion dollars to clean it up.

Third is the military buildup. Defense spending went from 158 billion to 300 billion. But he also cut taxes and didn't cut much else, so the national debt tripled from 900 billion to 2.8 trillion.

The modern connection is that Reagan basically reset what counts as a normal tax rate and a normal deficit. Those baselines are still in place today. Every argument about taxes or the national debt starts from assumptions Reagan set in 1981."

## LESSON 56 — COLD WAR
*(navigate to lesson-56.html)*

"Lesson 56 is about how Reagan and Bush ended the Cold War. Three examples.

First is the Reagan Doctrine — he funded anti-communist rebels in Nicaragua, Afghanistan, and Angola, and pushed the Strategic Defense Initiative, which was a space-based missile shield. The Soviets couldn't afford to match it.

Second is the summits with Gorbachev. They met four times between 1985 and 1988. The result was the INF Treaty in 1987 — the first arms deal that actually eliminated a whole category of nuclear weapons instead of just capping them. Over 2,600 missiles were destroyed.

Third is the fall of the Berlin Wall on November 9th, 1989, and the Soviet Union dissolving on December 25th, 1991. Bush Sr. deliberately didn't give victory speeches to avoid humiliating Gorbachev and triggering a hardliner backlash.

The modern connection is NATO expansion after 1991. Adding 14 new members pushed right up to Russia's border, and that's the backdrop for everything happening with Ukraine today. The Cold War didn't fully end — it just went quiet for a while."

## LESSON 59 — AMERICAN IDEALS
*(navigate to lesson-59.html)*

"Lesson 59 is about how the country defines and debates its ideals through the Supreme Court. I used three cases from 2019 to 2021.

First is Mahanoy v. B.L. in 2021 — a cheerleader posted a profanity-filled Snapchat off campus on a Saturday and got suspended from the team. The Supreme Court ruled 8 to 1 that her First Amendment rights were violated. First case ever on off-campus social media speech.

Second is Bostock v. Clayton County in 2020. Title VII bans employment discrimination because of sex. The Court ruled 6 to 3 that this includes sexual orientation and gender identity. Before this, LGBTQ workers in over half of U.S. states had no federal protection.

Third is Rucho v. Common Cause in 2019. The Court ruled 5 to 4 that federal courts can't touch partisan gerrymandering — it's a political question. But state courts still can, so the fight just moved there.

The modern connection is TikTok v. Garland in 2025. The Supreme Court ruled 9 to 0 that the government could force ByteDance to sell TikTok on national security grounds. Compare that to Mahanoy — a school lost 8 to 1 for punishing a Snapchat post, but the federal government won 9 to 0 restricting an entire platform. Same Constitution, very different outcomes depending on who's restricting speech and why."
